## 1. The fixes bar: no group box

The bar's group box belongs to the policy actions, and it keeps what was typed there. In the fixes it was read as "…or exclude this group instead": a group left in it **replaced every proposal and dropped the include half**.

Mihai's `INT-SG-D-WAVE-BAMSCA`, typed earlier for a policy include, did that. Had it been applied, BAMSCA would have left the old LAPS policy without joining the new one. That breaks the rule the waves mode exists for.

Now:

- **No group box in the fixes.** A single group on an old policy is still the policy bar's job, from 🗄 Old policies.
- **The bar says what the dry run will do**, from the same `fixWants()` the dry run uses. For example: "→ 5 groups out of 1 old policy · 5 waves into 1 new policy · 🧪 2 pilot assignments off".
- **Clearer note.** The note under an exclude that needs the include reads "joins the new policy in this plan". It used to read "+ included in the new policy", which contradicted the "not in it yet" below it.

## 2. 🧪 Pilots off, both sides

`cfg.pilotGroups` holds the four names; they are editable in ⚙️. `cfg.pilotGroupsOff` is one tick, on by default. The tick sits in ⚔️ next to the switch and in the ⚡ card.

The rule (`pilotRemovals`) runs over the state **after** the plan:

| Side | Comes off when | Stays when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A new policy's pilot **include** | Every wave of its kind is in it. That is every region, not only the ticked ones: pilot users in an unticked region would otherwise bounce back to the old policy. | An old policy it collides with still excludes that pilot. A pilot member outside a wave would get neither. |
| An old policy's pilot **exclusion** | Every wave of each colliding new policy's kind is out of it (twins count). | A new policy it collides with still includes that pilot. A pilot member outside a wave would get both. |

- **Each side needs the other**, so the check drops candidates until nothing changes.
- **What stays says why**, in the proposal (ⓘ) and in the plan's "Not in the plan" box.
- **Result:** a pilot member in a wave keeps the new policy through the wave. One outside a wave is back on the old policy until their wave has them.

Where it runs:

- **⚔️**, per fix and over the whole selection (`fixWants`).
  - Only the policies the selection touches are changed.
  - A pilot-only fix is a fix, for pairs whose waves are already in place.
- **⚡① and ⚡③**, wide: the colliding policies are in scope too, so the old side can go with the new.
  - ⚡③ is included because the usual order ①→③ would otherwise never complete a swap in one plan.
- **The bar's 🌊 Waves** (below), wide, on include and exclude.

## 3. 🌊 Waves in the policy bar

The target segment reads **Group · 🌊 Waves · All devices · All users**.

- With 🌊 Waves, each ticked policy gets the waves of **its** kind: a `- D -` policy the device waves, a `- U -` policy the user waves. The kind comes from the targets when the name says neither.
- The waves are the ones in the regions ticked in 🌊, with include, exclude or remove.
- Missing waves, and exclusions Intune refuses, are listed with the reason.
- The group box hides, and a note says "each policy's kind · <regions>".

## 4. 🕳 Left out counts Windows devices

- **The total:** the chip and its count are now Windows devices only: no country, no primary user, no Entra object, excluded.
- **The users with no Windows device** stay listed under their tile, marked "not counted". Their last column is now **Their groups**:
  - ✓ in `<user wave>` through the country group, or ✗ not in it yet, with the nest to do (⏳ for a pilot batch);
  - a Windows device they get later joins `INT-SG-D-<ISO3>` at the next 👥 read → Apply. The device groups are assigned groups kept by the sync, so a new device is picked up on the next read and apply.
