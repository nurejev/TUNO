## ⚔️ Fix with the waves

| Part | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| The switch | "Proposed fix excludes: 🌊 the waves / the new policy's groups" sits above the table. `cfg.fixWith` defaults to `waves` and is kept per tenant. |
| Waves mode (`wavesProposal`) | The waves of the new policy's kind (`- D -` → device waves), in the regions ticked in 🌊, are taken out of the old policy. Each step goes through the same support matrix, twin swap and include-to-remove rule as before (`stepFor`). |
| Same plan | A wave the new policy does not include yet is listed under "on the new policy, in the same plan". `dryRunFixes` composes the `add-include` on the new policy next to the `add-exclude` on the old one, all read fresh, backed up and read back in one plan. A wave never leaves the old policy ahead of the new one. This is ⚡① and ⚡③ for one conflict. |
| The rest | The new policy's own other groups (AVD, W365, pilot rings) are named and left alone. When the waves are in place the proposal says so, and names those groups if they still meet the old policy. |
| ⚡③ | Skips a waves-mode step that needs the include, with the reason ("⚡① first, or fix it in ⚔️"). Its rule stays: never ahead of the new policy. |
| Regions | Ticking a region in 🌊 re-derives the ⚔️ proposals. |
| Groups mode | Unchanged: the new policy's include groups, and a staged policy borrows the waves as planned. |

The verdict and "needs action" are unchanged. A conflict whose waves are in place but whose new policy still reaches the old one through, say, `WIN365-PROD` still counts. The proposal says why.

## Floating bars

The 👥 Wave members bar and the ⊘ Exclusions bar were already `position:sticky`, but `.list-card{overflow:hidden}` (and `overflow-x:auto` on narrow screens) pinned them to the end of their table. Their cards now allow overflow (`.mr-stickyhost`), so each bar floats at the bottom of the window while its card is on screen. Checked in Chromium at 1440×620: the bar's bottom sits 12 px above the window's.
